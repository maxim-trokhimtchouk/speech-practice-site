Common questions

How do we start?

Choose an animal, select one or more sounds, and tap Start practice. Each round has up to 10 random words containing all selected sounds. Say the displayed word while the microphone indicator is on. A parent confirms the pronunciation before moving on. Practice again starts another random round with the same sounds.

What if the device does not recognize the word?

Speak near the microphone in a quiet room. Recognition can miss children's speech and different accents. After an unsuccessful attempt, the app speaks an example and listens again. A parent can tap Parent: I heard the attempt to open the check manually, or skip the word. Recognition is not a clinical assessment.

What if listening is unavailable?

Check microphone and speech-recognition permissions for Speech Practice in Settings, then return and tap Try listening again. The app needs an available on-device English (US) speech recognizer. If it is unavailable on your device, the app does not send speech online; use the manual parent check instead. Apple manages the availability of system speech resources.

How do I stop the microphone?

Tap Cancel session, open the parent check, skip the current word, or leave the app. Skipping starts a new attempt for the next word; returning to an active practice screen can resume listening. The microphone is off during parent checks and spoken examples.

Which devices and language are supported?

Speech Practice supports iPhone and iPad running iOS or iPadOS 17 or later. The word collection and recognition use North American English. On-device listening availability depends on the device and its Apple speech resources. A parent should stay with the child during practice.

How do I report a problem?

An adult can email speechpractice.support@gmail.com. Include your device model, iOS/iPadOS version, app version, what you tried, and what happened. Please leave out children's names, voice recordings, photos, and medical information.

About speech practice

Practice together with a parent or caregiver. Speech Practice is an educational practice tool, not a diagnosis, clinical assessment, or replacement for advice from a qualified speech-language professional. The device recognizes words; a parent decides whether the pronunciation was clear.
